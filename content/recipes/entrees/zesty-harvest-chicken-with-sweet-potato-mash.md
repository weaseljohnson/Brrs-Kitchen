      - count: 1 tsp
        item: salt
      - count: 1/2 tsp
        item: ground pepper
      - count: 3 tsp
        item: ground cumin
      - count: 2-3 tsp
        item: paprika
      - count: 2 tsp
        item: garlic powder
      - count: 2 tsp
        item: onion powder
      - count: 2 tsp
        item: dried oregano
      - count: 1 whole
        item: lime, juiced
      - count: ¼ cup
        item: maple syrup (or 3 tbsp honey)
  - groupName: Sautéed Topping
    ingredients:
      - count: ½ whole

      - count: 15 oz
        item: canned corn
      - count: 15 oz
        item: canned beans (black or pinto)
      - count: ½ cup
        item: fresh cilantro, chopped

      - item: salt and pepper, to taste
  - groupName: Sweet Potato Mash
    ingredients:
      - count: 2 medium
        item: sweet potatoes, cut into 1-inch cubes
      - count: ½ cup
        item: butter
      - count: ½ cup
        item: heavy cream
      - item: salt and pepper, to taste
  - groupName: Yogurt Sauce
    ingredients:
      - count: 1 cup
        item: Greek yogurt (or sour cream, or mayo thinned out a bit with heavy cream)
      - count: ½ tsp
        item: ground cumin
      - count: ¼ tsp
        item: paprika
      - count: ¼ tsp
        item: garlic powder
      - count: ¼ tsp
        item: onion powder
      - count: 1/8 tsp
        item: ground coriander
      - item: zest of one lime
      - item: salt and pepper, to taste
directions:
  - title: Make the yogurt sauce
    body: Add all of the yogurt sauce ingredients to a bowl and mix until everything is incorporated. Taste for salt or anything else it may need. Cover and let it sit in the fridge until you are ready to eat.
  - title: Coat the chicken
    body: Pat the chicken thighs dry with a paper towel. Place them in a large bowl, drizzle with a couple tbsps of oil, add salt and pepper, cumin, paprika, garlic powder, onion powder, and oregano. Toss until every piece is evenly coated in the oil and spices. Set aside.
  - title: Boil the Potatoes
    body: Place the sweet potato cubes in a medium pot and add water until the potatoes are just covered. Add a generous sprinkle of salt and give it a quick stir with your fingers. Set the pot over high heat, and once it boils, turn the heat down to medium to keep a rapid simmer. Let it go untouched until the potatoes can be easily pierced through with a fork (about 20 mins), then drain and return them to the pot over medium-low heat.
  - title: Make your mash
    body: If you don't have a potato ricer or Tamis, use a hand mixer on the lowest setting to break up the potatoes, but only for a couple of seconds. Add the butter, cream, and salt and pepper, then mix and fold with a rubber spatula, scraping the bottom of the pot often so nothing sticks, until everything is smooth and incorporated. Taste to see if it needs more of anything, then turn the heat to low and cover to keep warm.
  - title: Sear the chicken
    body: While the potatoes are boiling, set a large skillet over medium heat and let it heat up until you can see faint wisps coming from the pan at eye level. Add the chicken in batches, arranging each piece so that there is visible space between them to avoid overcrowding and steaming rather than searing the meat.  Cook on one side for 2 to 3 minutes, or until golden brown, then flip and cook the other side until the internal temperature reaches 155°F. Set the chicken aside uncovered, on a cutting board or plate, to allow it to continue cooking from the inside.
  - title: Make the glaze
    body: Once all the thighs are cooked, turn the heat to medium-low. Add the lime juice and maple syrup (or honey), stir to combine, then let it simmer for a few minutes until it thickens slightly. You may need to turn the heat back up to medium. Chop the chicken into smaller pieces, add them back to the pan and toss to coat in the glaze. Remove everything from the pan and cover to keep warm while you make the topping.
  - title: Sauté the topping
    body: Turn the heat back to medium and add the onion to the same pan. There is no need to clean it out, but you may need a little more oil. Sprinkle with salt and pepper and cook for a few minutes, until the onion starts to turn translucent. Add the corn and season with salt, then let it simmer until the water has evaporated. Toss periodically until the kernels get some color and browning on the outside. Add the beans, season with salt and pepper, and toss periodically until the beans are heated through. Taste and add a little more lime juice and/or sweetener if you like, then remove from the heat, add the cilantro, and toss.
  - title: Serve
    body: 'Add the components to your plate however you like: potato mash on the side, or chicken on top of the mash, with the sauté on the side or as a topping for the chicken or potatoes. Drizzle the yogurt sauce over whatever you like. Everything tastes amazing together, so you really can''t go wrong. Enjoy!'
notes:
  - Salt as you go. With each new addition, sprinkle with salt. This is an important rule of thumb no matter what recipe you are making. It is much easier to add salt as you go than to try to add all the salt you need at the end, and your dishes will come out more evenly salted. Even so, always taste before serving. Exact measurements only take you so far, and we all have different preferences for salty or sweet, so don't be afraid to go outside the recipe to make it perfect for you.
  - Your pan needs to be hot enough to create a great sear on the meat. Wait until you see faint wisps coming from the pan at eye level before adding the chicken. If the pan is not hot enough, the chicken can cook through before it has a chance to sear, leaving it colorless, bland, and dry. A proper sear means great flavor and a juicier interior.
  - Why an internal temp of 155°F (71°C)? The FDA says 165°F (74°C), because at that point there is absolutely no risk, no matter how inexperienced the cook. But that also means your meat is woefully overcooked. Insert the bland dry and chewy chicken people are used to. Pro chefs cook poultry to about 155°F, then allow it to rest off heat for at least 5 minutes before cutting. This is called "Carryover cooking", and it allows the chicken to continue cooking from the inside whilst maintaining all of it's moisture. This technique leaves poultry perfectly cooked yet still moist, tender, and flavorful. If you don't have a proper thermometer, get one. You need it.
  - 'You can absolutely sauté the onion, corn, and bean mixture in a separate pan. I like cooking it in the same pan as the chicken and glaze because it picks up all those yummy flavors from before: the umami of the seared meat and the sweet citrus of the glaze.'
