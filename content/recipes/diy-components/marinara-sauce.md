We want aaalll the flavor in all the right places, and this sauce has it, as well as what has now become my irreplaceable secret ingredient. It''s so easy, and so so good.'

ingredients:
  - count: 3 tbsp
    item: oil (olive or avocado)
  - count: 1 medium
    item: yellow onion, diced
  - count: 1 tbsp
    item: Italian seasoning
  - count: 1 tbsp
    item: dried basil
  - count: ½ tsp
    item: celery seed
  - count: 4 whole
    item: garlic cloves, minced
  - count: 3 tbsp
    item: tomato paste
  - count: 28 oz
    item: can crushed tomatoes (or whole peeled San Marzano tomatoes, crushed by hand)
  - count: ¼ cup
    item: Worcestershire sauce
  - count: ¼ cup
    item: coconut aminos (TJ’s organic)
  - count: ¼ cup
    item: cane sugar
  - item: salt and pepper, to taste
  - count: 1 lb
    item: ground beef, cooked (optional, for a heartier sauce like with spaghetti)
directions:
  - title: Cook the aromatics
    body: Heat the oil in a deep medium or large pot over medium heat. Once the oil is hot, add the onions and stir to coat them in the oil. Sprinkle with salt and pepper, then add the Italian seasoning, dried basil, and celery seed. Stir and spread the onions out as evenly as possible. Stir periodically until the onions are translucent and starting to caramelize (pick up some color). Add the garlic, stir, and cook until fragrant, about 30 seconds. Add the tomato paste and mix until everything is thoroughly coated and pasty. If the onions look dry or thinly coated, add a little more paste. Cook for another minute or so.
  - title: Create the sauce
    body: Add the crushed tomatoes and mix everything together, scraping the bottom of the pot so any fond (browned bits) gets worked into the sauce. Add the Worcestershire sauce, coconut aminos, sugar, and salt and pepper to taste. These add flavor and dimension, and the sugar balances the acidity of the tomatoes. Taste and adjust the salt, sugar, or anything else it needs. For the heartier version, stir in the cooked ground beef once all of the sauce ingredients are incorporated.
  - title: Reduce the sauce
    body: Turn the heat to medium high to bring the sauce to a simmer, then lower it to medium-low to maintain. If you have time, go lower for a slow bubble. If you need the sauce sooner, keep the heat closer to medium for a more rapid simmer, which evaporates the water faster. Simmer for at least 30 minutes, or up to 2+ hours. The longer it simmers, the more concentrated the flavor gets, as the tomatoes release their water and all the components settle in, get to know each other, and infuse the sauce with their goodness.
notes:
  - The oil and tomato paste can each go up to 4 tbsp. Add more tomato paste if the onion mixture seems dry.
  - If the bottom of the pot looks browned after sauteing the onions and garlic, you can deglaze it with ¼ cup (60ml) of red wine vinegar before adding the tomato paste. Stir and scrape up the browned bits, then continue as written.
  - Use the optional ground beef for a heartier sauce, like for spaghetti. Cook it separately and add it after all the sauce ingredients are incorporated.
