cookTime: 45 min – 2+ hrs
yield: 5 cups
intro: A rich, savory marinara with a little tang and a little sweetness, built from scratch in one pot. Make it your own base for pasta, pizza, or anything that needs a good red sauce.
tags:
  - Sauces
  - Family Favorite

ingredients:
  - count: 3 tbsp
    item: oil (olive or avocado)
  - count: 1 medium
    item: yellow onion, diced
  - count: 1 tbsp
    item: Italian seasoning
  - count: 1 tbsp
    item: dried basil
  - count: ½ tsp
    item: celery seed
  - count: 4 whole
    item: garlic cloves, minced
  - count: ¼ cup
    item: red wine vinegar
  - count: 3 tbsp
    item: tomato paste
  - count: 28 oz
    item: can crushed tomatoes (or whole peeled San Marzano tomatoes, crushed by hand)
  - count: ¼ cup
    item: Worcestershire sauce
  - count: ¼ cup
    item: coconut aminos (TJ’s organic)
  - count: ¼ cup
    item: cane sugar
  - item: salt and pepper, to taste
  - count: 1 lb
    item: ground beef, cooked (optional, for a heartier sauce like with spaghetti)
directions:
  - title: Cook the aromatics
    body: Add the oil to a deep medium or large pot and set it over medium heat. Once the oil is hot (you will notice very faint wisps coming from it when you look at it at eye level), add the onions and stir to coat and spread them out as evenly as possible. Sprinkle with salt and pepper and stir again. Add the Italian seasoning, dried basil, and celery seed, then stir again. Stir periodically until the onions are translucent and starting to caramelize and pick up some color. Add the garlic and stir to incorporate, then let it cook until fragrant, about 30 seconds. Pour in the red wine vinegar and stir to deglaze, which means scraping up the browned bits stuck to the bottom of the pot. Add the tomato paste and mix until everything is thoroughly coated and pasty. You do not want it dry, or with just a thin coating that barely tints the onion mixture, so add a little more paste if it seems too dry. Once it is mixed in, let it cook for another minute or so.
  - title: Create the sauce
    body: Add the crushed tomatoes and mix everything together, scraping the bottom of the pot so that any fond (those browned bits) gets worked into the sauce. Add the Worcestershire sauce, coconut aminos, sugar, and salt and pepper to taste. These add flavor and dimension, and the sugar balances the acidity of the tomatoes. Taste and adjust, adding more salt, sugar, or anything else it needs. If you are making the heartier version, stir in the cooked ground beef once all of the sauce ingredients have been incorporated.
  - title: Reduce the sauce
    body: Turn the heat to medium high to bring the sauce up to a simmer, then lower the heat to medium-low to maintain it. If you have time, turn the heat low enough for a slow, bubbling simmer. If you need the sauce sooner, keep the heat closer to medium for a more rapid simmer, which helps the water evaporate faster. Let it cook and reduce for at least 30 minutes, or up to 2+ hours. The longer it simmers, the more concentrated the flavor gets, as the tomatoes release their water and all the components settle in, get to know each other, and infuse the sauce with their goodness.
notes:
  - The oil and tomato paste can each go up to 4 tbsp. Add more tomato paste if the onion mixture seems dry.
  - Use the optional ground beef for a heartier sauce, like for spaghetti. Cook it separately and add it after all the sauce ingredients are incorporated.
