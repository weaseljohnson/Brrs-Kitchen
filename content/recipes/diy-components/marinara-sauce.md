cookTime: 45 min – 2+ hrs
yield: 5 cups
intro: 'This is my kind of perfect marinara sauce: it''s rich, umami, beautifully sweetened with just enough tang, with a touch of Italian herbal essence to round out the flavor. Gone are the days of subpar jarred marinara, or versions that lean too acidic, sweet, or bland. We want aaalll the flavor in all the right places, and this sauce has it, as well as what has now become my irreplaceable secret ingredient. It''s so easy, and so so good.'

ingredients:
  - count: 2 tbsp
    item: oil (olive or avocado)
  - count: 1 medium
    item: yellow onion, diced
  - count: 1 tbsp
    item: Italian seasoning
  - count: 1 tbsp
    item: dried basil
  - count: ½ tsp
    item: celery seed
  - count: 3 whole
    item: garlic cloves, minced
  - count: 3 tbsp
    item: tomato paste
  - count: 1 28 oz
    item: can crushed tomatoes (or can of whole peeled San Marzano tomatoes, crushed by hand)
  - count: 3 tbsp
    item: Worcestershire sauce
  - count: 3 tbsp
    item: Trader Joes Organic Coconut Aminos (secret ingredient!)
  - count: ¼ cup
    item: cane sugar (or regular is fine)
  - item: salt and pepper, to taste
  - count: 1 lb
    item: ground beef, cooked (optional, for a heartier sauce like with spaghetti)
directions:
  - title: Cook the aromatics
    body: Add the oil to a deep medium/large pot and set it over medium heat. Once the oil is hot, add the onions and stir to coat in the oil. Sprinkle with salt and pepper, add the Italian seasoning, dried basil and celery seed, the stir and spread out the onions as evenly as possible. Stir periodically until the onions are translucent and starting to caramelize/pick up some color. Add the garlic and stir to evenly disperse/incorporate, then let it cook until fragrant, about 30 seconds. Add the tomato paste and mix until everything is thoroughly coated and pasty. You do not want this to be dry, so add a little more paste if it seems like the onions are too thinly coated. Once everything is thoroughly incorporated, let it cook for another minute or so.
  - title: Create the sauce
    body: Add the crushed tomatoes and mix everything together, scraping the bottom of the pot so that any fond (browned bits stuck to the bottom) gets worked into the sauce. Add the Worcestershire sauce, coconut aminos, sugar, and salt and pepper to taste. These add flavor and dimension, and the sugar balances the acidity of the tomatoes. Taste and adjust, adding more salt, sugar, or anything else it needs. If you are making the heartier version, stir in the cooked ground beef once all of the sauce ingredients have been incorporated.
  - title: Reduce the sauce
    body: Turn the heat to medium high to bring the sauce up to a simmer, then lower the heat to medium-low to maintain it. If you have time, turn the heat low enough for a slow bubble simmer. If you need the sauce sooner, keep the heat closer to medium for a more rapid simmer, which helps the water evaporate faster. Let it cook and reduce for at least 30 minutes, or up to 2+ hours. The longer it simmers, the more concentrated the flavor gets, as the tomatoes release their water and all the components settle in, get to know each other, and infuse the sauce with their goodness.
notes:
  - Use a splatter guard over your simmering sauce to avoid a splattered stove top, and possibly you!
  - Use the optional ground beef for a heartier sauce, like for spaghetti. Cook it separately and add it after all the sauce ingredients are incorporated.
  - I know the secret ingredient is very specific, and that not everyone has access to a Trader Joe's! I like the one from TJ's because it's more sweet than it is salty, whereas I've noticed that other coconut amino brands (granted I haven't tried all of them) vary in flavor and can be too salty for my taste (after all, it is made to be a substitute for soy sauce). I'm sure you can use other brands of coconut aminos, but I would recommend adding much less (maybe 1 tbsp?) and going from there, or even taste testing before adding to gauge how much you may or may not want to add.
