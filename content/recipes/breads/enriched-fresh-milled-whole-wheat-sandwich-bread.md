ingredients:
  - count: 540g (4½ cups)
    item: fresh home-milled flour (2 parts hard red wheat, 1 part hard white wheat), divided
  - count: 180ml (¾ cup)
    item: warm water, for the main mix
  - count: 60ml (¼ cup)
    item: warm water (105–110°F / 40–43°C), for blooming the yeast
  - count: 300ml (1¼ cups)
    item: milk, room temperature or lukewarm
  - count: 9g (1 tbsp)
    item: active dry yeast
  - count: 113g (⅓ cup)
    item: honey, divided (a small drop goes in the yeast mixture)
  - count: 2 large
    item: eggs, room temperature
  - count: 15g (2½ tsp)
    item: fine sea salt
  - count: 76g (⅓ cup)
    item: butter, melted
  - item: extra melted butter, for brushing the hot crusts
directions:
  - title: Mix the autolyse
    body: In the bowl of a bowl-lift KitchenAid mixer, combine the ¾ cup (180ml) warm water, the 1¼ cups (300ml) milk, and the 540g (4½ cups) flour. Attach the paddle, raise the bowl, and mix on Speed 1 for about 1 minute, just until all the dry flour is hydrated into a soft, wet mash.
  - title: Rest the dough
    body: Remove the paddle and scrape down the sides of the bowl. Cover the bowl tightly and let it rest on the mixer base for 45 to 60 minutes. This lets the sharp outer bran absorb moisture and soften so it doesn't cut the gluten strands during kneading.
  - title: Bloom the yeast
    body: About 10 minutes before the rest ends, stir together the ¼ cup (60ml) warm water (105–110°F / 40–43°C), the 1 tablespoon yeast, and a small drop of honey taken from the ⅓ cup. Let it sit for 5 to 10 minutes, until frothy.
  - title: Prep the enrichments
    body: In a separate bowl, whisk the 2 eggs and the remaining honey until smooth. Keep the salt and the melted butter separate for now.
  - title: Add the wet ingredients
    body: Lower the mixer bowl and switch to the dough hook. Pour the frothy yeast mixture and the egg and honey mixture over the dough, then raise the bowl. Mix on Speed 2 for 3 to 4 minutes, until the wet ingredients are fully drawn into the dough.
  - title: Add the salt
    body: Sprinkle the 2½ teaspoons salt evenly over the dough. Keep kneading on Speed 2 for another 2 to 3 minutes to build the first gluten structure.
  - title: Knead in the butter
    body: With the mixer running on Speed 2, slowly stream in the ⅓ cup melted butter. Knead for 4 to 6 more minutes, until the butter is completely absorbed and the dough forms a smooth ball around the hook.
  - title: Check the texture
    body: Whole-wheat dough should be soft, stretchy and slightly tacky, but clean to the touch. If it sticks heavily to the sides of the bowl (common in humid weather), add more flour 1 tablespoon at a time until it clears the sides.
  - title: First rise
    body: Transfer the dough to a greased bowl, cover tightly, and set it in a warm spot. Let it rise for 60 to 90 minutes, until fully doubled in size.
  - title: Shape the loaves
    body: Grease two 8.5 x 4.5 inch (22 x 11 cm) metal loaf pans with butter or shortening. Turn the dough out onto a lightly floured surface and divide it into two equal portions. Press each portion into a flat rectangle to push out the big gas pockets, then roll it tightly from the short end into a taut log, pinching the final seam shut firmly. Place each loaf seam-side down in a prepared pan.
  - title: Second rise
    body: Cover the pans lightly with a damp cloth or greased wrap. Let the loaves rise at room temperature until the center of the dough crowns about 1 inch (2.5cm) above the rim of the pan. Watch closely so they don't over-proof.
  - title: Bake
    body: During the last 15 minutes of proofing, preheat the oven to 350°F (175°C). Bake for 30 to 35 minutes, until deep golden brown or until the internal temperature reaches 190–200°F (88–93°C).
  - title: Brush and cool
    body: Right after baking, brush the hot tops with melted butter for a tender crust. Let the loaves sit in the pans for 5 minutes, then move them to a wire rack. Cool completely, at least 2 hours, before slicing, or the inside can turn gummy.
notes:
  - If the dough is sticky, you can work in up to 1 more cup of flour, about 1 tablespoon at a time.
  - Brushing the hot crusts with melted butter keeps the crust tender.
  - Store in a bread bag.
